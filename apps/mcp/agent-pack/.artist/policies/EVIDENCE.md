# Evidence

Assume the likely shape of a page, then verify it. When the assumption does not
hold, widen the search — never report the assumption's failure as a finding.

A page usually names its subject near the top, and usually puts its date, venue
and headline facts there too. Looking there first is right: it is cheap and it
is where the answer normally is. But an artist's notebook is not a database, and
the next page will be laid out differently — the date halfway down, the venue in
a sentence, the subject only in the title, or none of it where it was expected.

## Assume, verify, escalate

1. Look where the fact is likely to be. A sketch of a page opening, the title,
   the first heading.
2. Check that what came back actually answers the question.
3. Where it does not, escalate: read the whole page. Where the whole page does
   not answer it either, escalate to saying so.

The step that matters is the third. A cheap look that failed is not evidence of
absence — it is evidence of nothing, and the difference is the whole point.

## A failed assumption is never a finding

- "No date near the top" is not "no date recorded". Read the page first.
- "The opening does not mention the venue" is not "the venue is unrecorded".
- "The two page openings look unalike" is not "these are different projects".
- "The sketch showed no owner" is not "this task is unowned".

Every one of those becomes a false gap if reported without widening first, and a
false gap sends the musician to redo work they have already done. That is the
opposite of what this exists for.

## Say which happened

- Where the assumption held, say nothing about it. It is machinery.
- Where the search widened, that is machinery too, unless it changed the answer.
- Where even the wide search found nothing, that is not machinery: report it as
  unrecorded, and say how far the search went, so "not recorded on the page" is
  not mistaken for "not recorded anywhere".

## Boundaries

- Never conclude from a cheap look alone when the conclusion is negative. A
  positive finding may come from a sketch; an absence may not.
- Never widen silently past the agreed scope. Reading the rest of a page is
  ordinary; reading another notebook is not, and needs the musician to ask.
- Never fill what the wider search did not find. Missing stays missing.
